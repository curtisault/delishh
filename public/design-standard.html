00 Scope, and what to put in which file

This standard covers a single-operator recipe archive: a place to keep recipes, find them again, and print them. It is derived from the cryovault aesthetic — acid-Y2K graphic realism: flat, printed-looking vector graphics applied with total confidence to a grounded physical world — and it inherits that project's discipline wholesale while replacing its subject and its affect.

Codify it as separate documents with clean ownership, because a single DESIGN.md always collapses into a pile of unenforceable opinions. The split below is what keeps a rule findable a year later.

FileOwnsMaps to
DECISIONS.mdEvery ruled mechanic, constant and formula, newest-first dated timeline; dated amendment blocks override the prose beneath themThe spec of record
DESIGN-REQUIREMENTS.mdLook, feel, voice, color, type, motion§§01–05, 10
DESIGN-PRINCIPLES.mdLayout and structure, independent of dressing§§06–08
PRINT.mdThe issued sheet: ink budget, break law, traceability§09
RECIPE-SCHEMA.mdThe ten blocks as a data contract, and what is required vs. optional§06
LEXICON.mdControlled vocabulary — approved and banned word fields, canonical units, surface grammars. Enforced in CI§11

One governing rule, adopted from the source project and worth more than any styling decision in this document: if build reality contradicts a decided rule, you write the dated amendment first and change the code second. Never diverge silently.

01 The premise, and the one inversion that makes it work

The one-line vibe: a test kitchen's issuing office — clinical, acid-bright, and completely certain — where every recipe is a controlled document, and the only warm thing on the page is your own handwriting.

The institution

Give the archive an in-fiction issuer whose voice the interface speaks in. This standard uses PROOFWORKS CULINARY STANDARDS — proof as in dough, as in a printer's proof, as in proven — under the mark «NOTHING LEAVES UNTESTED». Pick your own; the requirement is that one exists.

The aesthetic's whole thesis is institutional branding that outlived the institution. Without an institution, the placards and serial plates are cosplay — you are just putting hazard stripes on a lasagna. The fiction costs one paragraph and then pays for every string in the product, because you never again have to decide what tone an empty state takes.

Hold the source project's boundary: the issuer is an in-fiction company only. It is not the name of your site, your repo, or your domain. The moment the two collapse, the joke becomes a brand and the brand stops being funny.

The inversion

Cryovault runs the flat institutional register toward dread — the horror is in the mundanity, and a line like ANOMALOUS READ ACTIVITY ACKNOWLEDGED. NO ACTION REQUIRED. is doing the work of a jump scare. A recipe archive runs the identical machinery toward the opposite end, and this single move is what keeps the aesthetic from being creepy about dinner.

Clinical menace becomes clinical care. A kitchen that documents its own failures, states the temperature it actually means, and admits which revision was too pale is not being cold — it is being more generous than any recipe blog has ever been. Every formal device survives the translation. Only the affect is retargeted.

And the register earns the archive's one human voice. Because the system is deadpan everywhere else, a note in a serif, in sentence case, in the first person — Mum went by smell, not temperature — lands with a force it could never have on a page that was already chatty. The institutional voice is the setup; your handwriting is the payoff. Protect that ratio; it is the emotional engine of the whole design.

02 Design pillars

2.1 · Graphic realism

Every element reads as a printed or silk-screened artifact: a jar label, a spec plate, a safety placard, a shipping tag, a tamper seal, a nutrition panel. Flat color, hard edges, no bevels, no soft shadows. Depth comes from layering and overlap, never from lighting. If it could not plausibly be screen-printed onto a stainless bench, it does not belong.

2.2 · Acid on cold neutral

The field is cold and neutral; the signal is acid, and the ratio is roughly 90% neutral to 10% accent. Acid is applied like tape or warning paint — never ambient. When everything glows, nothing does. Note that this is a genuine discipline problem for a food site, where the instinct is to make the food the color. Resist it: the food supplies warmth, in one photograph per document (§6), and the interface stays cold around it.

2.3 · Clinical care

The tone is a test kitchen's internal documentation: precise, procedural, unhurried, and entirely unsentimental about what goes wrong. The UI never raises its voice and never sells. PAST 190 °C IT DOES NOT COME BACK.

2.4 · The recipe is an object

A recipe is not a post and not a card in a feed. It is a controlled document with a serial, a revision number, a tested-on date, a yield, and a revision history. It can be superseded. It can be reissued. Treat it with the gravity of a part drawing, and the interface writes itself.

2.5 · Objects inform you of their use

The verb is stenciled on the thing. A step states its own temperature and its own doneness cue on its own line — no tooltip, no hover, no "see notes below." A filter chip states what it excludes. A pan is named with its diameter and its material because those change the result. If an element needs external explanation, its design has failed.

2.6 · Designed, never broken — and the wear clause

The interface is immaculate by intent and stays immaculate. No simulated coffee rings, no torn-paper edges, no distressed textures, no "vintage recipe card." That is nostalgia cosplay and it is the single most common failure mode of recipe-site design.

The screen never ages. The sheet does. Wear is real or it is not present — and the printout is where this system lets reality in. Your kitchen supplies the stains; the software's job is to look like the day it was issued and to reprint on demand.

03 Reference points, and the anti-references

The Designers Republic / WipeoutFake institutions treated with real brand discipline; aggressive grotesk typography
Y2K hardware & softwareTranslucent plastics as color reference only; chunky progress bars; installer-layout density

Explicitly not

  • Not a food blog. No origin story above the fold, no SEO preamble, no "jump to recipe" button — which exists only to apologize for a layout that should not have been built.
  • Not rustic farmhouse. No kraft paper, twine, chalkboard, reclaimed wood, or handwriting fonts. Warmth comes from the content, not the texture.
  • Not dark-moody restaurant photography. Our light is laboratory light and the photograph is a record, not a seduction.
  • Not a minimal Scandinavian recipe app. Our spareness is the result of a density discipline, not the whole idea. We are allowed eight type sizes on one surface if every one of them means something.
  • Not vaporwave, and never ironic. The institution believes in itself completely.

04 Color — an acid names a physical process

This is the section that decides whether the aesthetic transfers or merely gets imitated. In cryovault an accent color names a currency; the color carries information, which is why the palette never reads as decoration. Port the rule, not the mapping.

Each acid names a thermal or biological state of the food. Not a cuisine, not a category, not a meal slot. The instant you color-code "Italian" against "Thai," the system is decoration again and the discipline is gone.

TokenNamesAppears on
--acid-voltThe actionable — the primary verb, the active step, the scale control, the print buttonOne per surface, and it is the loudest thing there
--acid-cyanCold chain — chill, freeze, cure, set, raw, hold at 4 °CStorage blocks, freezer rows, thaw instructions
--acid-orangeHeat and hazard — fry, sear, sugar work, smoke point, hot fat, anything that burns youThermal-class marks, temperature tells, the equipment warnings
--acid-magLive — ferment, culture, proof, brine, anything where something is alive in the jarFailure modes, live-culture holds, the "check it daily" clause

Magenta is the anomaly color in the source project — "something woke up." A sourdough starter is literally that. This is the mapping that convinced me the translation was real rather than clever: the food world supplies a true referent for every accent the game invented.

Rules

  • One acid dominates per surface; a second may cameo; never three. A recipe document takes the acid of its dominant method.
  • Acid never lands on a quantity. Amounts, temperatures and times are set in ink or frost — the dazzle lives in the labels and the structure, never in the data. This is the rule that makes a dense page trustworthy.
  • Information is never color-only. A thermal class carries its word as well as its mark. Required for color-blind readers, and it also happens to be exactly what makes the black-and-white printout work (§9) — one rule, two payoffs.
  • Frost and ice live in the neutral family (white-cyan), so acid always reads as human-applied marking rather than as the food itself.

The field: light and dark are strata, not themes

Cryovault darkens its field as the player descends, swapping text and accent tokens per stratum. There is no descent in a recipe archive, so re-host that machinery on the viewer's color scheme: light is the lit surface — clinical armor-plastic with ink text; dark is the deep stratum — near-black with acid at full strength. The token names and the swap mechanism are identical; only the trigger changed.

The consequence you must respect: volt-on-light fails contrast at text size. On a light field, acid appears as blocks and fills with ink text — never as small colored type. Mint darkened text variants (--volt-tx, --cyan-tx) for the cases that need colored words, and verify every pair at 4.5:1. This document is built that way; compare its two themes.

05 Type, and the measurement ladder

Four voices, strictly cast. A glyph's typeface tells you what kind of information it is, every time.

Specimen · the four voices

Display — Archivo Expanded 700 · plates, titles, section stencilsSALTED CARAMEL
Procedure — Inter 500 · every word you read while cookingButter in, all at once. It will foam to the rim of the pan; beat it flat.
Data — JetBrains Mono · every number that means something175–180 °C · 200 g · 6–9 MIN · REV 3
Human — Instrument Serif 400 · your notes, and nothing elseThe first two revisions were too pale.

Two deliberate changes from the source casting. First, the body voice is promoted. In the game it is deliberately invisible micro-copy with no personality; here it carries procedure that gets read at arm's length in bad light with wet hands, so it is chosen for legibility at distance and given real size. Second, the human voice is promoted too — rare-by-contract in the game, recurrent here. Its contract changes from "rare" to never used for procedure. And the game's fifth voice is cut: a fifth voice is a cost, and this product has nothing for it to say.

The measurement ladder

Quantities need as much specification as color does, and almost no recipe site has any. Rule the ladder once and enforce it in code:

  • Mass is authoritative; volume is a convenience and is printed second, dimmed. Grams as integers; no 142.5 g.
  • Fractions are glyphs (¾ tsp), never decimals, for volume. Decimals are for mass and temperature only.
  • Scaling rounds per unit — to 5 g above 100 g, to ¼ tsp, to whole grinds — and never produces a fractional egg, can, or clove. When it would, the system emits a note instead: SCALED ×1.5 — USE 2 EGGS, HOLD BACK 20 g OF THE WHITE. An honest note beats a false number.
  • Temperatures are dual and consistently ordered (180 °C / 356 °F), authoritative unit first.
  • The clock is never alone. Every duration carries its tell: 6–9 MIN · UNTIL THE EDGES RUN CLEAR. Time is the least reliable variable in any kitchen, and a recipe that gives only a number is withholding the thing you actually need. This is §2.5 applied to duration.
  • Tabular numerals everywhere, and a running timer must never shift layout as it counts.

06 The recipe document — ten blocks, fixed order

A controlled document has a fixed form. Every recipe carries the same blocks in the same order, so you learn where to look exactly once and never hunt again. Blocks may be empty; they may not be reordered.

#BlockCarries
1Title plateName, serial, revision, tested-on date, yield, active and total time, thermal class
2Specimen plateOne photograph. One.
3Bill of materialsIngredients, grouped by sub-preparation, mass-first, scalable
4EquipmentNamed with the dimensions and materials that change the result
5ProcedureNumbered steps, each with its own tell
6TolerancesThe critical limits — the temperatures and states that decide success
7Failure modesWhat goes wrong, what causes it, whether it can be saved
8Holding & storageKeeps, freezes, reheats — the cold chain
9The noteYours. Serif, sentence case, first person, unedited
10Revision historyWhat changed and when

Above the fold is the bill of materials, not prose. A returning cook needs quantities; a first-time cook needs to know what to buy. Neither needs a paragraph. The note is at the bottom on purpose — it is the reward for having read the document, not the introduction to it.

Blocks 6 and 7 are the ones conventional recipe sites omit, and they are the reason to build this at all. A published failure mode is the highest-value sentence in any recipe.

The photograph

One per document, and treated as a record photograph — a specimen plate, not a glamour shot. Clamp it into the cold neutral family or render it as a coarse halftone: dither is already in the motif vocabulary, it keeps the payload tiny, and it stops a warm photo from blowing the 90/10 acid budget on its own.

The photograph stops competing with the data and starts reading as evidence. If a recipe has no photograph, the plate is simply absent — never a placeholder, never a grey box.

07 The index — a manifest, not a gallery

The index is a dense ledger of rows, not a grid of photographs. A photo grid optimizes for discovery-by-appetite, which is what you want when selling recipes to strangers. A personal archive is used for retrieval — you already know the thing exists and you want it in four seconds — and retrieval wants scannable text. The photographs live on the documents, one each, where they are evidence.

The method mark rides the left rail of the serial, so the thermal class is legible before you have read a word. The excluded row stays visible and states which filter removed it.

Rules

  • Filters narrow; they never hide the reason. An excluded recipe remains on the page, tagged with the filter that excluded it. Lockout tags, not disappearance — this is the source project's "locked content is tagged, not greyed out" rule, and it matters more here, because a silently-vanished result teaches you to distrust your own archive.
  • Search is a query line, monospace, focused on load. Matches are knocked out as ink blocks, not washed in yellow.
  • The facets come from the document blocks (§6), which is why the schema is worth writing first: thermal class, active time, total elapsed including holds, equipment required, keeps or freezes, provenance.
  • Active time and total elapsed are different facets and both are shown. A twelve-hour cure is not a twenty-minute recipe, and collapsing the two is the most common lie in recipe software.
  • Default sort is last tested, not alphabetical. The archive's own working order is more useful than the dictionary's.
  • Zero results is designed copy with an action, in voice: NO RECORDS MATCH. NEAREST BY TIME ▸. A dead end is a design failure, not an edge case.

08 Cook mode — the arm's-length constraint

Reading a recipe while cooking is a different activity from browsing one, and it imposes constraints no other screen in the product has: you are two to three feet away, your hands are wet or greasy or full, the light is bad, and you are under time pressure with something on the heat.

  • Body type at 20px equivalent or larger, and the step number larger again. This is the one place the type scale steps up rather than down.
  • No hover-only information, anywhere. There may be no pointer at all, and if there is one it is attached to a dirty hand. Everything a step needs is printed on the step.
  • Steps stamp when complete; they never grey out. A completed step must stay fully legible — you will re-read it to check what you already did. Tagged, not disabled.
  • Tap targets at 2.75rem minimum, scaling with text.
  • Hold the screen awake while cook mode is open, and say so on screen. Silent power management is a system lying about its status.
  • Scaling is set before you start and displayed permanently in the header. A scaled document that does not say it is scaled is dangerous.

09 The issued sheet — print is the native output

This is not a concession to an old habit. The entire aesthetic is built out of printed artifacts — decals, placards, serial plates, registration marks — and a recipe archive is one of the very few products whose users genuinely print. Everything the style already believes about paper finally gets to be literally true. Design the sheet first and let the screen be the proof of it.

The format

  • One sheet, portrait. If a recipe cannot fit, that is a signal about the recipe, not about the paper. Where it must break: never between a heading and its table, never inside a step, never orphan the failure modes.
  • Margins in physical units, sized for the sheet to be handled at the edge and for a clip to hold it.
  • Body at 11pt minimum, step numbers at 14pt — larger than the screen, because the sheet sits on a counter and you are standing.

Ink discipline

  • Black plus one screen tint. That is the whole budget. Acid does not survive a monochrome laser and wastes color toner on a page that will be thrown away. A volt fill becomes a 100% black block with knockout text; hazard bands become 45° hatching; the thermal class prints as a reversed word.
  • No reversed body type. Knockout survives on a heading and fills into an unreadable smear at 9pt after one photocopy.
  • The photograph prints as a coarse halftone or not at all. A 300-dpi photo on a home laser is a grey smear that eats the toner budget.

Traceability — the fiction paying rent

The footer carries the serial, the revision, the scale factor it was printed at, the date it was pulled, and a short URL with a data matrix. A sheet found in a drawer in three years should be able to tell you what it is and how out of date it is. This is the single most useful thing the institutional conceit produces, and no food blog has ever done it.

The sheet is designed to be destroyed. Reprinting is the intended lifecycle, not a failure — which is exactly why it must be cheap in ink, one page, and traceable back to its current revision. It also resolves §2.6 cleanly: the software never simulates wear, because the real artifact is out there getting ruined properly.

10 Motion — the ledger opens empty

Cryovault governs motion with a numbered register: every piece of ambient movement on the page needs its own dated entry written before it ships, and a retired entry banks no permission for the next one. Adopt the mechanism; start the list at zero.

A game wants ambience. A reference document read with a hot pan in one hand wants to hold absolutely still. The marquees, the drifting rails, the breathing glow — none of it ports, and saying so explicitly is worth more than any of it.

  • Zero ambient motion. Nothing loops, drifts, breathes, or pulses.
  • Sanctioned exception 01 — the step timer. A running duration is functional readout, not decoration: the number is the information, so it is allowed to change. It runs on tabular numerals and must not shift layout by a pixel as it counts.
  • State changes the user caused may transition — under 150 ms, stepped easing, no springs, no bounce, no overshoot. Machinery indexing, not jelly.
  • Calm mode kills everything site-wide and prefers-reduced-motion implies calm. New motion is authored inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) so reduced-motion users never have motion defined at all, rather than merely overridden.

11 Lexicon — the controlled vocabulary

Put the word rules in their own file and enforce them in CI. Of everything in this standard, the vocabulary is the highest-leverage and the fastest to erode, because copy is written in a hurry and nobody reviews a tooltip.

BannedBecause
delicious, yummy, perfect, amazing, incredibleEnthusiasm substituted for information. The document does not have opinions about whether you will enjoy it.
game-changer, crowd-pleaser, the best ___ everMarketing register. The institution does not sell.
simply, just, easy, foolproofThey minimize a difficulty the reader is currently having. "Simply" has never once helped anyone.
toss, whip up, throw togetherImprecise verbs where the procedure needs a real one.
Emoji; exclamation marks in system voiceWrong register, and they break the type casting.
The origin-story preambleThe note block (§6.9) exists for exactly this, at the bottom, where it is a reward.

Approved field, and the grammars

Procedural verbs with defined meanings (fold, beat, swirl, render, slake); tolerances; tells; failure modes; holds. Canonical unit spellings, fixed once. Surface grammars, fixed once and reused everywhere:

Two standing carve-outs

  • The note is exempt entirely. First person, sentence case, feeling, digression — all permitted, none edited toward house voice. The linter does not read it. Sanding down the one human voice on the page to match the machine would destroy the thing the design exists to frame.
  • The system never lies about status. "Ready in 20 minutes" on a recipe with an overnight cure is a lie even though every individual number is true. Total elapsed always includes holds. The company is deadpan; it is not dishonest.

12 Hard constraints — never waived

  • Dense but never broken. No overlapping text, no clipped quantities, no unreadable state, at any density.
  • WCAG AA for all functional text, measured on its actual background, in both themes. Bold is not legible; contrast is.
  • Information is never carried by color alone, on screen or on paper.
  • ≤ 3 Hz flashing; calm mode; prefers-reduced-motion respected.
  • User text scaling respected — no px font sizes, breakpoints in rem, and the never-broken bar holds at 200% zoom and at a raised browser default font. These are two different mechanisms and both must work.
  • Reading and printing work with JavaScript off. A recipe is a document. Scaling and cook mode may be enhancements; the text, the quantities, and the print stylesheet may not be.
  • Fast on a phone in a kitchen. The acid layer is CSS and SVG, never image payloads; at most one photograph per document, lazily loaded.
  • Self-hosted assets only. No font CDNs, no third-party analytics on a document you may want to read in ten years.

13 Governance, and where to start

The source project's documentation habits are as transferable as its palette, and they are the reason its aesthetic survived a year of building:

  • Amendments are dated blocks, not rewrites. A new ruling is appended with its date and overrides the prose beneath it. The superseded reasoning stays readable, because "why did we stop doing it that way" is the question you will actually have.
  • Bench alternatives, then rule from the bench. Build the three candidate treatments side by side on identical content behind a flag, look at them, pick one, and record what the two rejected ones were. Deciding from a description is how you end up with the thing you already imagined.
  • Constants live in one file — tokens for color and type, and a single module for anything that is a rule rather than a rendering. Retune there and nowhere else.
  • The word linter runs in the test suite, not in a review checklist.

The first five things to build

  • 1 · The token file. Both themes, all four acids, every text variant, with the measured contrast ratios in a comment beside each pair.
  • 2 · The recipe schema. Ten blocks, fixed order, required vs. optional. Everything else in the product is a view of this.
  • 3 · One real recipe, end to end. A hard one, with genuine tolerances and failure modes. It is your bench specimen forever.
  • 4 · The print stylesheet — before the browse page, not after. If the sheet is right, the screen has very little left to get wrong.
  • 5 · The lexicon and its linter, while the corpus is small enough that fixing every violation takes an afternoon.
