## Equipment

- Heavy 20 cm saucepan, pale interior — a dark pan hides the colour, which is the only tell that matters here
- Probe thermometer, reading to 200 °C
- Heatproof spatula
- Jar, 400 ml, warmed

## Ingredients

- 200 g caster sugar
- 90 g unsalted butter, cubed, cold
- 120 g double cream, held at 40 °C
- 6 g flaky salt

## Steps

1. Warm the cream and hold it there. Cold cream into hot sugar is the
   single cause of seizing, and it is the one failure in this recipe
   you can prevent entirely.
   cue: 40 °C · HOLD UNTIL STEP 6

2. Sugar into the dry pan in one even layer. No water, and no stirring
   — swirl the pan instead. A spoon drags crystals up the side and
   drops them back in.
   cue: MEDIUM HEAT · DRY PAN

3. Melt undisturbed until the edges run clear and the pool turns
   straw. Swirl once to even it out, then leave it alone again.
   cue: 6–9 MIN · UNTIL THE EDGES RUN CLEAR

4. Take it to deep amber and the first thread of smoke, then off the
   heat. The colour is the tell, not the clock — a pale caramel is
   sweet and flat, and there is no way to add the flavour back later.
   cue: 175–180 °C · DEEP AMBER, FIRST SMOKE

5. Butter in, all at once. It will foam to the rim of the pan. Beat it
   flat and keep beating until the surface goes glossy.
   cue: OFF THE HEAT · FOAMS HARD

6. Cream in a thin stream, beating throughout. Salt last, off the
   heat, stirred through rather than beaten.
   cue: POURS LIKE WARM HONEY

## Watchpoints

- Cream at 40 °C when it goes in. Below about 20 °C the caramel seizes into a lump.
- 175–180 °C is the window. The pan carries heat past the number, so pull it at 175 °C if your hob runs hot.
- Past 190 °C it is bitter, and bitter is the one state on this page that cannot be corrected.

## Rescues

- **Grainy, or crystallized to sand** — A sugar crystal fell back into the pool from the side of the pan. Add 30 g water, return it to a low heat, and re-take it to 180 °C. It comes back clear.
- **Seized into a lump** — The cream was too cold. Low heat, keep beating, give it two full minutes before you decide it has failed. It comes back.
- **Split, with butter pooling on top** — Beaten too slowly when the butter went in. Off the heat, add 15 g cream and beat hard from the centre out.
- **Bitter** [terminal] — Past 190 °C. Nothing recovers it. Pour it out, wash the pan, start again with the thermometer in the pool this time.

## Keeps

- Jar it warm, cap it cold — a lid on a hot jar sweats and thins the top centimetre.
- Fridge at 4 °C, keeps 14 days.
- Do not freeze. It separates on thaw and no amount of beating brings it back.
- To loosen: 20 g at a time in a warm pan, or thirty seconds in a bain-marie. Never a direct microwave — it scorches from the middle.

## Note

Mum's pan was aluminium and she went by smell, not temperature — she'd
say it's ready when it smells like it's about to be too late. I never
understood that until I burned three batches trying to be careful.

The third go is that sentence, in numbers. The first two were too
pale, and pale caramel is just sweet — all of the sugar and none of
the point.
