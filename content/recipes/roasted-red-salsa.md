## Equipment

- Sheet pan lined with foil — the tomatoes leak and the char sticks
- Broiler, or a dry cast iron skillet on high if the oven has none
- Blender, or a food processor

## Ingredients

- 6 Roma tomatoes
- 2 jalapeños
- ½ white onion, in thick slices
- 3 cloves garlic, unpeeled
- ⅓ cup cilantro, loosely packed
- 1 tbsp lime juice, fresh
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ⅓ cup water

## Steps

1. Put the tomatoes, jalapeños, onion and the unpeeled garlic on the
   foil-lined pan and slide it under a hot broiler. Turn once or twice
   as they blacken. Pull the garlic the moment it darkens — it goes
   first, and burnt garlic is bitter all the way through.
   cue: 8–12 MIN · BLACKENED IN SPOTS, TOMATOES SOFT

2. Let everything cool until you can handle it. Peel the garlic and
   stem the jalapeños, taking the seeds out for a milder salsa. Leave
   the charred tomato skins on; they are the flavour.
   cue: COOL ENOUGH TO HANDLE · SKINS ON

3. Pulse the charred vegetables with the cilantro, lime juice, salt
   and water until mostly smooth with a little texture left. Stop
   before it turns to purée.
   cue: SHORT PULSES · MOSTLY SMOOTH, A LITTLE TEXTURE

4. Let it sit so the flavours settle, then taste and adjust the salt
   and lime. Serve warm or at room temperature.
   cue: 15 MIN · THEN TASTE FOR SALT AND LIME

## Watchpoints

- The garlic darkens before anything else and turns bitter if it goes black. Pull it early and leave the rest.
- Blackened in spots, not blackened. The char is a flavour on top of the tomato, and a tomato that is all char tastes of ash.
- Pulse, never run the blender. A smooth salsa is a sauce; this one keeps a little texture.

## Rescues

- **Bitter** — The garlic burnt, or too much of the skin went black. Add another charred tomato if there is one, or a pinch of sugar and more lime; if it is strongly bitter, start again and pull the garlic earlier.
- **Watery, separating in the bowl** — Too much water, or very wet tomatoes. Simmer it in a pan for five minutes, stirring, and it tightens and deepens.
- **Too hot to eat** — The jalapeños were fierce, or the seeds went in. Add a charred tomato or two and more lime; heat spreads out but does not leave.
- **Flat, tastes of nothing** — Under-salted, nearly always. Salt a pinch at a time and wait a minute between; it also needs the fifteen-minute rest before it tastes like itself.

## Keeps

- Fridge, 5 days, in a jar. It is better on the second day, once the char has settled into the tomato.
- It thickens cold. Loosen with a spoon of water and bring it back to room temperature before serving.
- Freezes for 3 months; it separates on thawing, so stir it or give it a short pulse.

## Note

This is the table salsa at Lupe Tortilla in Houston, the one that
arrives with the chips before you have ordered: loose, smoky, a
little hot, more tomato than anything else. Most roasted salsas are
thicker than this; the water is there on purpose, because the
restaurant's is thin enough to pour.

Swap one jalapeño for a serrano if you want more heat. A dry cast
iron skillet on high does the charring as well as a broiler, and on a
gas stove it is faster.
