## Equipment

- 30 × 5 cm anodized deep-dish pan — a 25 cm cast-iron skillet is the good substitute, a cake pan the last resort
- Rolling pin
- Parchment, two sheets, for pressing the sausage patty
- Lowest oven rack — the pan is the stone

## Ingredients

### Buttercrust dough

- 450 g all-purpose flour
- 35 g fine yellow cornmeal — for the snap
- 2¼ tsp instant yeast
- 1½ tsp salt
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 300 g warm water, 40 °C / 105 °F
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
- 3 tbsp corn oil or neutral oil

### Butter layer — for the lamination

- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, very soft
- 2 tbsp corn oil plus butter, for the pan

### Sausage patty — goes in raw

- 450 g bulk Italian sausage, lean — not sweet
- 1 tsp fennel seed, crushed
- ½ tsp black pepper
- ¼ tsp red pepper flakes

### Sauce — uncooked, barely seasoned

- 800 g can whole peeled tomatoes, San Marzano or 6-in-1
- 1 tsp salt
- ½ tsp sugar
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1 garlic clove, grated — optional

### Assembly

- 450 g whole-milk low-moisture mozzarella, in 5 mm slices
- ⅓ cup parmesan or pecorino, grated

## Steps

1. Whisk the flour, cornmeal, yeast, salt, and sugar. Add the water,
   melted butter, and oil, and knead 5 minutes by hand or 3 in a
   mixer, until smooth but still soft and slightly tacky. This is a
   fat-heavy dough, closer to pastry than to bread — do not chase a
   tight windowpane; tender, not chewy.
   cue: 10 MIN · SMOOTH, SOFT, SLIGHTLY TACKY

2. Rise in an oiled bowl, covered, until doubled — 90 minutes in a
   cold kitchen. Only one real rise here; deep-dish crust is
   supposed to be short and crumbly, not airy.
   cue: 1 H · DOUBLED

3. Roll the dough into a 38 × 25 cm rectangle. Spread the soft
   butter edge to edge, roll it up the long way into a tight log,
   then coil the log into a spiral like a snail. Wrap and chill.
   This is the whole point of the card — butter rolled into layers
   is what makes the crust flake and taste like a pie shell instead
   of bread.
   cue: 30 MIN CHILL · LOG, THEN COIL

4. Oil and butter the pan heavily. Flatten the coil, roll it to a
   38 cm round, and press it into the pan and 4.5 cm up the sides.
   If it fights back, rest it 15 minutes and finish pressing.
   Press, never stretch — thin walls slump during the bake, and the
   wall is what holds a pound of cheese.
   cue: 10 MIN · PRESSED UP THE SIDES, NEVER STRETCHED

5. Drain the packing liquid, then crush the tomatoes by hand into a
   bowl. Stir in the salt, sugar, oregano, and garlic — and do not
   cook it. The sauce cooks on top of the pie for 35 minutes;
   pre-cooking gives it a flat, stewed flavor by the time it comes
   out.
   cue: 5 MIN · CRUSHED BY HAND, RAW

6. Shingle the mozzarella slices over the bottom and right up
   against the walls, leaving no gaps. The cheese layer is a
   gasket — it seals the crust against the sauce, which is why the
   bottom stays crisp under all that liquid.
   cue: 5 MIN · SHINGLED TIGHT, NO GAPS

7. Mix the sausage with the fennel, pepper, and flakes. Press it
   between the parchment sheets into a 30 cm round, peel off the
   top sheet, lay it over the cheese, and patch any tears. Crumbles
   leave holes for sauce to drain through; one continuous patty
   renders down into a single meat layer, the way Lou's does it.
   cue: 5 MIN · ONE PATTY, TEARS PATCHED

8. Spread the tomatoes over the sausage, dust with the parmesan,
   and bake on the lowest rack at 220 °C / 425 °F until the walls
   are deep brown and the center bubbles. Rest 10 minutes before
   cutting — the rest is not optional; cut it hot and the whole pie
   runs.
   cue: 30–35 MIN · WALLS DEEP BROWN, CENTER BUBBLING

## Watchpoints

- Built upside down on purpose: cheese on the crust, sausage on the cheese, raw tomatoes on top. Reorder it and the crust boils.
- Block mozzarella, sliced — never pre-shredded. The starch coating makes the layer grainy and lets sauce find its way through to the crust.
- The sausage goes in raw and has to render out completely — the center needs the full bake even when the rim looks done.
- Cast iron runs hotter than anodized. In the skillet, pull it at 30 minutes.

## Rescues

- **Rim browning early** — Tent it with foil at 25 minutes and keep baking; the center is not done.
- **Walls slumped** — The dough was stretched thin instead of pressed, or the pan was under-buttered. Rest the dough mid-press next time and keep the walls at full thickness.
- **Soggy bottom** — Gaps in the cheese gasket, or a middle rack. Shingle to the walls and bake on the lowest rack.
- **Sausage in hard crumbles** — It was pre-cooked. The patty goes in raw; the oven does the rendering.

## Keeps

- Freeze the parts, never a raw assembled pie — the crust soaks grease and bakes greasy.
- Dough: after the coil, wrapped tight in plastic then foil — 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and give it an hour on the counter before pressing.
- Sauce: raw, in a flat quart bag — 6 months. It can go onto the pie still half-frozen.
- Sausage: pressed into its 30 cm round between parchment, rolled loosely — 2 months. It lays onto the cheese straight from the freezer.
- Mozzarella: always fresh. Frozen mozzarella turns grainy and will not seal into the gasket.
- Baked pie: by the slice, 2 months. Reheat on a rack at 175 °C / 350 °F until the center is hot.

## Note

After Lou Malnati's Chicago Classic. Only about twenty minutes of
this card is real work — the rest is the rise, the chill, and the
bake — so a Tuesday pie is genuinely possible if Sunday did the
mixing. Ask a butcher counter for plain bulk Italian sausage and
season it yourself; anything labeled sweet or maple fights the
tomatoes, and links in casings will not press into a clean patty.
