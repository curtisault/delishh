## Equipment

- A 1 litre jar with a lid that seals, washed in hot water
- Small saucepan, non-reactive — an aluminium pan turns the brine metallic

## Ingredients

### The vegetables

- 400 g cucumbers, cut into 1 cm coins
- 3 cloves garlic, peeled and bruised with the flat of a knife
- 2 sprigs dill

### The brine

- 240 ml cider vinegar
- 240 ml water
- 20 g caster sugar
- 18 g fine salt
- ¾ tsp black peppercorns
- ½ tsp yellow mustard seed
- ¼ tsp dill seed

## Steps

1. Pack the cucumber coins into the jar with the garlic and the dill
   pushed down the sides. Pack them tight enough that they hold each
   other under the brine.
   cue: THE JAR SHOULD LOOK FULL

2. Bring the vinegar, water, sugar and salt to a bare simmer, stirring
   until the grains are gone. Do not let it boil hard — you drive off
   the vinegar and the brine goes flat.
   cue: 3–4 MIN · UNTIL THE GRAINS HAVE GONE

3. Off the heat, stir in the peppercorns, mustard seed and dill seed.
   The brine wants to be warm when it goes on, not hot.
   cue: 70 °C · WARM, NOT STEAMING

4. Pour the brine over to cover, pressing the coins down until no
   cucumber is proud of the surface. Seal, cool on the counter, then
   into the fridge.
   cue: EVERYTHING UNDER THE BRINE

5. Leave them alone. They are sharp and raw at a day, properly sour
   and crisp at two, and best from three.
   cue: 2 D MINIMUM · SNAP, NOT BEND

## Watchpoints

- Anything above the brine line goes soft and then goes off. A small glass weight or a folded cabbage leaf solves it.
- Warm brine, not boiling. Hot brine cooks the coins and they turn to rubber in a week.
- Cucumbers only ever get softer. If they are bendy going in, they will be mush coming out.

## Rescues

- **Soft and bendy after two days** — The brine went on too hot, or the cucumbers were old. Neither is recoverable as a pickle, but the coins are still good chopped into a potato salad.
- **Cloudy brine** — Usually the salt, if it was iodised or had anti-caking agents in it. Harmless, and it clears nothing — next batch, use fine sea salt.
- **Tastes flat, not sour** — The brine boiled. Drain it off, bring a fresh brine to a bare simmer, and pour it back over cold.
- **Slimy, with a sour-milk smell** [terminal] — Something got in that is not lactic acid. Do not taste it to check. The whole jar goes.

## Keeps

- Fridge at 4 °C, keeps 6 weeks sealed.
- Once opened, 2 weeks — and the coins soften from the day you open it.
- Do not freeze. The cell walls burst and they thaw as pulp.
- The brine is reusable exactly once, for a second batch, within a week.

## Note

I made these for years with boiling brine because that is what the jar
on my grandmother's counter said, and for years they were fine on
Tuesday and sad by Friday. It was my brother who said the obvious
thing out loud: she was canning them to keep for a year, and I am
putting them in a fridge for a fortnight. Different problem, different
temperature.

Three days is genuinely the sweet spot. I have never once managed to
leave them that long.
