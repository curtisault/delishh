## Equipment

- 23 × 33 cm baking dish
- Wide skillet or Dutch oven, for the filling
- Large pot and a masher or ricer, for the potatoes

## Ingredients

### Filling

- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 900 g ground beef
- 1 yellow onion, diced
- 2 carrots, diced
- 2 ribs celery, diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 3 tbsp tomato paste
- 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 1 cup stout
- 2 cups beef broth
- 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 cup frozen peas

### Cheddar mash

- 1.5 kg russet potatoes, peeled, in 5 cm chunks
- 6 tbsp butter
- ¾ cup whole milk, warm
- 1 egg yolk
- 2 cups sharp cheddar, shredded, divided
- 1 tsp kosher salt

## Steps

1. Cover the potatoes with cold salted water, bring to a boil, and
   cook until a knife slides through with no resistance.
   cue: 15–20 MIN · KNIFE SLIDES THROUGH

2. Meanwhile, heat the oil over high and brown the beef in two
   batches. Crowd the pan and it steams grey instead of browning,
   and the gravy loses its depth.
   cue: 10 MIN · IN BATCHES, REAL CRUST

3. Lower to medium. Cook the onion, carrots and celery in the fat
   until soft, then the garlic and tomato paste until the paste
   darkens.
   cue: 8 MIN · SOFT, PASTE DARKENED

4. Return the beef, sprinkle in the flour and stir for a minute.
   Pour in the stout and let it bubble down by half, scraping the
   bottom.
   cue: 3 MIN · STOUT HALVED, NO RAW FLOUR

5. Add the broth, Worcestershire, thyme, salt and pepper. Simmer
   until the gravy coats a spoon thickly. Off the heat, stir in the
   peas, and taste — it should read slightly over-seasoned, because
   the freezer dulls salt.
   cue: 15 MIN · COATS A SPOON

6. Drain the potatoes well and let them steam dry in the pot for a
   minute. Mash with the butter, warm milk, egg yolk, half the
   cheddar and the salt until smooth.
   cue: STEAMED DRY · SMOOTH, NO LUMPS

7. Spread the filling in the pan and let it cool until it stops
   steaming. Spoon the mash over in dollops from the edges in,
   spread it to seal against the sides, then rough the surface with
   a fork and scatter the rest of the cheddar.
   cue: MASH SEALED TO THE EDGES · FORKED TOP

8. To freeze: cool completely, then wrap. To bake now: 200 °C /
   400 °F, uncovered, until bubbling at the edges and the ridges
   are deep golden.
   cue: 35 MIN · EDGES BUBBLING, RIDGES GOLDEN

## Watchpoints

- The mash goes on a filling that has stopped steaming — on a hot one it sinks and slides.
- Seal the mash to the pan walls; any gap lets gravy bubble up and over the top.
- Steam the potatoes dry before mashing; wet mash freezes icy and bakes up gluey.

## Rescues

- **Gravy thin and runny** — Not simmered long enough. Return it to the pot and reduce before assembling; once it is under the mash it cannot be fixed.
- **Mash gluey** — Overworked, or mashed in a food processor. Use a masher or ricer and stop at smooth.
- **Top pale after the time is up** — Run it under the broiler for 2–3 minutes, watching the whole time.

## Keeps

- Freezer, assembled and unbaked: 3 months. Plastic pressed onto the mash, foil over the top; label before it goes in.
- From frozen: 200 °C / 400 °F, 60 minutes covered, then 30 uncovered, until the center is hot.
- From thawed overnight in the fridge: 35 minutes uncovered.
- Baked leftovers: fridge 4 days.

## Note

Technically a shepherd's pie is lamb and this is beef, which makes it
a cottage pie — I'll fight about it if asked. The egg yolk in the
mash is the trick; it's what turns the top into a crust instead of a
blanket. Any dark beer works for the stout, or swap it for more broth
and an extra spoon of Worcestershire.
