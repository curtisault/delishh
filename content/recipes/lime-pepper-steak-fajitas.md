## Equipment

- Cast iron skillet or a grill, wide enough that the pieces lie flat without touching — crowded steak steams and never chars
- Second skillet for the onions, so they are done before the steak goes on
- Zip-top bag or a shallow dish, for the marinade
- Board with a groove, and a sharp knife — the slicing is half the recipe

## Ingredients

### The marinade

- ½ cup lime juice, fresh
- ½ cup soy sauce
- 1 tbsp black pepper, coarsely ground — coarse, not fine; it is in the name
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp neutral oil

### The steak

- 900 g skirt steak, outside skirt if you can find it, trimmed

### The onions

- 2 tbsp butter
- 1 white onion, large, sliced
- ½ tsp kosher salt

### To serve

- 8 flour tortillas, large and thin
- 2 limes, cut into wedges

## Steps

1. Whisk the lime juice, soy sauce, pepper, garlic and oil together.
   It is a thin marinade and it is meant to be — there is nothing in
   it that needs to cling.
   cue: THIN AND SHARP · PEPPER VISIBLE

2. Cut the steak into pieces that will lie flat in the pan. Bag it
   with the marinade, press the air out, and refrigerate. Thirty
   minutes is enough and two hours is the limit: past that the lime
   starts cooking the surface and the meat turns mealy.
   cue: 30 MIN – 2 H · COLD, NEVER LONGER

3. Melt the butter in the second skillet over medium heat. Add the
   onion and the salt and cook, stirring now and then, until soft and
   deep golden. Keep them warm; they are done before the steak starts.
   cue: 20–25 MIN · SOFT AND DEEP GOLDEN

4. Lift the steak out and pat every face dry. A wet surface boils
   before it browns, and the marinade has done its work already.
   cue: BONE DRY · MARINADE DISCARDED

5. Heat the cast iron until it smokes. Lay the steak in without
   touching, and leave it alone until the first side is charred,
   then turn once.
   cue: 3–4 MIN A SIDE · WELL CHARRED, MEDIUM-RARE

6. Move the steak to the board and let it rest. Warm the tortillas in
   the emptied pan, or straight over a flame, while it does.
   cue: 5 MIN · JUICES SETTLED

7. Slice thin across the grain — skirt has a long, obvious grain, and
   cutting with it gives you rope. Pile the steak over the onions and
   serve with the tortillas and the lime wedges.
   cue: THIN SLICES · ACROSS THE GRAIN

## Watchpoints

- Two hours in the marinade is a limit, not a target. Lime juice is acid, and acid cooks meat; past two hours the surface goes grey and the texture goes mealy.
- The pan must be smoking before the steak goes in. Skirt is thin and cooks through fast, so the only way to get a crust and a pink middle at once is heat the pan cannot lose.
- Pieces must not touch. Work in two batches rather than crowd the pan; crowded steak steams in its own juice.
- Across the grain, thin. The same steak sliced with the grain is chewy enough to be a different recipe.

## Rescues

- **Grey all the way through, no crust** — The pan was not hot enough, or the steak went in wet. Serve it anyway sliced thin; for the next batch, dry the pieces and wait for smoke.
- **Charred outside, raw inside** — Thick pieces of inside skirt, or an outside skirt with a thick end. Finish the thick pieces in a 200 °C oven for a few minutes, or slice them and return the slices to the pan for a few seconds.
- **Mealy, falling apart on the board** [terminal] — Too long in the lime. The texture does not come back. Chop it fine and serve it as taco filling, where it will be fine, and mark the marinade time next time.
- **Chewy, in long strands** — Sliced with the grain. Turn the remaining pieces ninety degrees and slice across it; what is already cut can be cut again, shorter.

## Keeps

- Cooked steak, sliced, keeps 3 days in the fridge in a sealed container with the onions over it.
- Reheat fast in a hot skillet with a spoon of water, lid on for a minute, lid off to let it sizzle. The microwave turns it to leather.
- The onions alone keep a week and are worth doubling.
- The marinade is used once and discarded. It has been on raw meat and it does not keep.

## Note

This is the plate from Lupe Tortilla in Houston — the one that arrives
on a sizzling platter with the onions already on it. The restaurant's
own version is a secret, and every copy on the internet agrees on the
same three things: lime, soy, and a great deal of coarse black
pepper. This is that, with the timing worked out.

Outside skirt is the classic cut and it is harder to find than inside
skirt; flank or flap works if you cannot get either. For something
closer to the restaurant finish, brush the sliced steak with a little
melted butter mixed with lime juice and more pepper before it goes
on the platter.
