## Equipment

- 23 × 13 cm metal loaf pan
- Parchment strip, long enough to hang over both long sides — the sling is how the loaf comes out in one piece
- Instant-read thermometer — done is 96 °C / 205 °F in the center
- Wire rack

## Ingredients

### Wet

- 3 very ripe bananas — 1¼ cups mashed
- 113 g unsalted butter, melted
- 150 g dark brown sugar, packed
- 2 large eggs, room temperature
- 80 g sour cream or full-fat Greek yogurt
- 2 tsp vanilla extract

### Dry — no sifting, no second bowl

- 220 g all-purpose flour, spooned
- 1 tsp baking soda
- ¾ tsp fine salt
- ½ tsp ground cinnamon

### Top

- 1 tbsp turbinado or demerara sugar

### Instead of the walnuts — optional, pick one

- 150 g chocolate chips, coated in a spoonful of the flour
- 75 g toasted pepitas — seeds, not nuts
- or nothing at all; it is excellent plain

## Steps

1. Oven to 175 °C / 350 °F. Butter the loaf pan and lay in the
   parchment strip so it hangs over both long sides. Banana bread is
   heavy and wet and will not turn out of a pan cleanly — the sling
   is the exit.
   cue: 5 MIN · PAN BUTTERED, SLING IN

2. Fork the bananas in a large bowl until mostly smooth, leaving a
   few lumps — about 1¼ cups of mash. A blender makes banana soup,
   and a soup-textured batter bakes flat and dense; the lumps turn
   into pockets of jammy fruit.
   cue: 3 MIN · MOSTLY SMOOTH, A FEW LUMPS

3. Into the same bowl: melted butter, brown sugar, eggs, sour cream,
   vanilla. Whisk hard until it looks glossy rather than curdled.
   Melted butter instead of creamed is the whole texture decision —
   creaming builds a fine cakey crumb; melted keeps it dense and
   moist.
   cue: 3 MIN · GLOSSY, NOT CURDLED

4. Add the flour, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon straight onto the
   wet. Fold with a spatula until no dry flour shows — about twelve
   passes — and stop early. Every stroke past that develops gluten;
   overmixed banana bread bakes tough, tunnels through the middle,
   and domes into a crack.
   cue: TWELVE STROKES · NO DRY FLOUR, STOP EARLY

5. Scrape into the pan, level the top, and scatter the turbinado
   edge to edge. Coarse sugar does not dissolve — it sits on the
   surface and bakes into a crackly lid, the only crunch this loaf
   needs.
   cue: COARSE SUGAR EDGE TO EDGE

6. Leave the filled pan on the counter while the oven finishes
   coming up to temperature. The flour hydrates and the soda gets a
   head start — same batter, better rise and a more even crumb, for
   ten minutes of nothing.
   cue: 10 MIN · REST WHILE THE OVEN FINISHES

7. Bake 55 to 65 minutes, tenting with foil at 40 if the top is
   running ahead. Done is a skewer with moist crumbs and no wet
   batter, or 96 °C / 205 °F in the center — banana bread reads done
   on the outside long before it is, and the crack on top should
   look set and dry, not glossy.
   cue: 55–65 MIN · SKEWER MOIST, NEVER WET

8. Cool 15 minutes in the pan, then lift it out by the sling and
   give it 30 more on the rack. The crumb is still setting — cut it
   hot and the slices compress into something gummy, and the loaf
   goes stale twice as fast.
   cue: 45 MIN · ALL THE WAY COOL BEFORE THE KNIFE

## Watchpoints

- Underripe bananas are not a small compromise — they are a different, blander bread. Yellow with a few freckles is not ready: the skins should be more black than brown, the fruit sweet-smelling through the peel, the flesh mashing with no resistance.
- Never hold the batter. The soda starts reacting the moment it hits the banana and sour cream; a batter left overnight bakes up flat and soapy. Bake it, then keep the loaf.
- Metal pan, spooned flour. Glass runs the edges dark before the center sets, and scooped flour packs 20% heavy.

## Rescues

- **Sank in the middle** — Underbaked, or the soda is old. Test the soda in a spoonful of vinegar: it should erupt, not fizz politely.
- **Top cracked open, sides pulled away hard** — The oven runs hot. Drop to 165 °C / 325 °F and add ten minutes next time.
- **Bananas black but rock hard** — They went in cold from ripening. Bake them unpeeled on foil at 150 °C / 300 °F, 15–20 minutes, until black and glossy — then cool completely or they will scramble the eggs.
- **Short a banana** — Two bananas plus 60 g of applesauce covers it. Never thin the batter to make up the volume.

## Keeps

- Counter, 4 days: cool completely, then wrap the whole loaf in plastic — or cut-side down on a board under a bowl. Never the fridge; refrigeration stales quick breads faster than room temperature does.
- Freezer, 3 months: slice first, stack with parchment between, and bag flat. A whole loaf freezes fine, but thawing commits to the entire thing at once.
- Warm frozen slices in foil at 150 °C / 300 °F, 10–12 minutes.
- Other pans: two 20 × 10 cm loaves, 40–45 minutes; 12 muffins at 190 °C / 375 °F, 18–22 minutes; a 20 cm square, 30–35 minutes — all at the loaf temperature unless noted.

## Note

The loaf gets its sugar, its moisture, and all of its flavor from the
fruit, so the banana rules are the recipe. Ripening too slow: a paper
bag on top of the fridge with an apple in it for a day or two. From
the freezer: thaw in a bowl and pour the brown liquid in with them —
that is concentrated sugar and it belongs in the batter. No nuts is a
position, not an omission; the pepitas exist for people who want the
crunch without the walnut.
