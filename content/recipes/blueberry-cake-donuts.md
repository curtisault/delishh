## Equipment

- Heavy pot at least 13 cm deep, never more than a third full of oil
- Probe or candy thermometer — the oil holds 185 °C / 365 °F, and holding it is the recipe
- Spice grinder or blender, for the freeze-dried berries
- 7.5 cm ring cutter with a 2.5 cm center
- Wire rack — donuts drain on a rack, not paper

## Ingredients

### Dry

- 440 g all-purpose flour
- 1 cup freeze-dried blueberries, one 28 g bag, ground to powder
- 4 tsp baking powder
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ¾ tsp nutmeg, freshly grated — non-negotiable

### Wet

- 135 g granulated sugar
- 2 eggs, room temperature
- 1 egg yolk
- 3 tbsp butter, melted and cooled
- ¾ cup buttermilk, cold
- 1 tsp vanilla
- 1 lemon, zested

### The berries — wild, and still frozen

- ¾ cup frozen wild blueberries, the small ones
- 1 tbsp flour, to coat

### Fry and glaze

- 2 l neutral oil, canola or peanut
- 2 cups powdered sugar
- ⅓ cup freeze-dried blueberries, ground
- 3–4 tbsp whole milk or cream
- 1 tsp lemon juice

## Steps

1. Blitz the freeze-dried blueberries to a fine powder and whisk the
   powder into the flour with the baking powder, salt, and nutmeg.
   This is where the flavor and the purple come from — concentrated
   blueberry with none of the water, seasoning the crumb instead of
   wetting it.
   cue: 2 MIN · TO A FINE PURPLE POWDER

2. Beat the sugar with the eggs and yolk until pale and thick, then
   beat in the melted butter, the buttermilk, vanilla, and lemon
   zest. Whipping the eggs and sugar first is what gives a cake
   donut its lift; baking powder alone leaves them squat.
   cue: 2 MIN · PALE AND THICK

3. Add the dry to the wet and fold with a spatula until no dry
   streaks remain. It will be very soft and sticky — that is right.
   Overworked dough turns tough and greasy in the fryer; a shaggy,
   sticky mass fries up tender.
   cue: STOP THE SECOND IT COMES TOGETHER

4. Tumble the frozen berries with the tablespoon of flour and fold
   them in with about six strokes. Do not thaw them and do not mash
   them — frozen berries stay whole through mixing and cutting,
   where thawed ones bleed the dough grey and wet.
   cue: SIX STROKES, BERRIES STILL FROZEN

5. Press the dough into a flat disc between two sheets of parchment,
   about 2.5 cm thick, and refrigerate. The single most important
   step: cold dough cuts cleanly, holds its shape, and spends less
   time absorbing oil. Warm dough gives you greasy blobs.
   cue: 2 H MINIMUM · OR OVERNIGHT

6. On a well-floured counter, roll the cold dough to a scant 1 cm.
   Cut rings, lay them on a floured sheet, and freeze them while the
   oil heats — the short freeze firms the cut edges so they do not
   slump going in. Re-roll the scraps once and no more; the third
   roll is always tough.
   cue: 15 MIN · ROLL, CUT, FREEZE THE RINGS

7. Heat 5 cm of oil to 185 °C / 365 °F and fry three at a time,
   flipping once when the underside is deep gold. Three at a time is
   the rule — crowding crashes the oil below 170 °C, which is
   exactly how donuts come out heavy and slick. Drain on the rack.
   cue: 90 S A SIDE · DEEP GOLD, ONE FLIP

8. Whisk the powdered sugar, ground berries, milk, and lemon juice
   to a thick pourable glaze. Dip the donuts face-down while still
   warm to the touch. Hot donuts melt the glaze straight off and
   cold ones will not take it — warm is the five-minute window in
   between.
   cue: GLAZE WARM · THE FIVE-MINUTE WINDOW

## Watchpoints

- Fresh blueberries do not go in, ever. A fresh berry is about 85% water; at frying temperature it bursts, the steam blows a crater in the crumb, and the rest spits out of the pot. Freeze-dried carries the flavor, frozen wild carries the fruit.
- All-purpose flour, not cake flour. "Cake donut" names the crumb and the leavening, not the flour — AP has enough protein to survive the drop into hot oil, and straight cake flour tears and drinks grease.
- The oil holds 185 °C. Watch the thermometer between batches and wait for it to come back.
- Two inches of oil in a deep pot, never more than a third full. Dry every tool that touches it, keep a lid within reach, and lower the rings in away from you.

## Rescues

- **Heavy and slick with oil** — The oil crashed, from crowding or from dough that went in warm. Fry fewer at a time, and give the rings their fifteen minutes in the freezer.
- **Grey, wet crumb** — The berries thawed or got mashed on the way in. Fold frozen, six strokes, and stop.
- **Tough rings** — The third roll of the scraps, or an overworked dough. Twice is the limit.
- **Glaze slid straight off** — They were still hot. Wait for warm-to-the-touch and dip again.

## Keeps

- Best within two hours; acceptable for a day under a towel. Past that, split them, butter the cut sides, and toast them in a skillet.
- Cut rings freeze raw on a floured tray, then bag — one month. Fry straight from frozen at 180 °C / 355 °F, about 2 minutes a side; the lower oil gives the center time.
- Never freeze a fried donut. It thaws damp and the glaze weeps.

## Note

Freshly grated nutmeg is the flavor that makes a cake donut taste
like a cake donut instead of a fried biscuit — it is the one spice
worth buying whole. If frying is off the table, loosen the dough
with another quarter cup of buttermilk, pipe it into a donut pan,
and bake at 205 °C / 400 °F for 10–12 minutes until springy, butter
brushed on before the glaze. Be honest with yourself about the
result: those are small cakes. Good ones, but not the same thing.
