## Equipment

- 23 × 33 cm baking dish — or two 20 cm squares; a strata is a weekend thing, and one pan usually does it
- Wide skillet, for the sausage
- Instant-read thermometer — done is 74 °C / 165 °F in the center, checked

## Ingredients

### Base

- 450 g bulk breakfast sausage
- 1 yellow onion, diced
- 12 cups day-old sourdough, cubed — sourdough or a sturdy country loaf; see Watchpoints
- 3 cups sharp cheddar, shredded
- 4 scallions, sliced

### Custard

- 12 large eggs
- 3 cups whole milk
- 1 tsp dry mustard
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1½ tsp salt
- dash hot sauce

## Steps

1. Brown the sausage, breaking it into small pieces as it cooks.
   Add the onion until soft, then drain the fat and let it cool —
   warm sausage starts cooking the eggs when the custard pours
   over.
   cue: 10 MIN · BROWNED, THEN COOLED

2. If the bread is not properly stale, dry the cubes on a sheet
   pan at 150 °C / 300 °F. Fresh bread cannot absorb custard; it
   goes to mush around the edges instead.
   cue: 15 MIN · DRY CUBES, NOT TOASTED

3. Whisk the eggs, milk, mustard, pepper, salt, and hot sauce
   until completely uniform — no ribbons of white left.
   cue: UNIFORM, NO RIBBONS OF WHITE

4. Layer: half the bread, half the sausage, half the cheese, half
   the scallions. Repeat.
   cue: BREAD · SAUSAGE · CHEESE, TWICE

5. Pour the custard evenly over and press down with your palms
   until every cube is wet. Cover and refrigerate at least 4
   hours, ideally overnight. The soak has to happen before
   freezing — a frozen strata cannot absorb anything.
   cue: 4 H MINIMUM · EVERY CUBE WET

6. Freeze here — wrap the soaked pan. Or bake at 175 °C / 350 °F,
   covered 40 minutes, uncovered 20, to 74 °C / 165 °F in the
   center.
   cue: 40 MIN COVERED + 20 · 165 °F CENTER

7. Rest before cutting. The custard needs to set or it slumps on
   the plate.
   cue: 10 MIN · SET, NOT SLUMPING

## Watchpoints

- Sourdough or a sturdy country loaf only. Sandwich bread dissolves, and the pan turns into bread pudding.
- The soak comes before the freezer, always. Overnight is the recipe; four hours is the floor.
- Shortest freezer life in the whole set — egg custard gets watery and weeps past about two months.

## Rescues

- **Watery and weeping** — Frozen too long, or the spinach in a meatless version went in wet. Two months is the wall; squeeze greens dry.
- **Mush at the edges, dry in the middle** — Fresh bread, or an uneven pour. Stale cubes, and press until every one is wet.
- **Slumped on the plate** — Cut too soon. Ten minutes of rest is part of the bake.

## Keeps

- Freezer, unbaked but fully soaked: 2 months. Plastic pressed flat, foil over the top; label before it goes in.
- From frozen: 175 °C / 350 °F, 80 minutes covered plus 25 uncovered. From thawed: 40 plus 20. Done is 74 °C / 165 °F in the center, checked.
- Good cold the next day, straight from the fridge.

## Note

Serve with fruit, coffee, and hot sauce. For a meatless pan, swap
the sausage for a pound of mushrooms and a bunch of wilted,
well-squeezed spinach — squeezed dry, or it waters the custard. Two
square pans beat one big one here: bake one for the weekend, freeze
the other.
