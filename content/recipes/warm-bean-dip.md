## Equipment

- Medium saucepan
- Blender, or a stick blender in the pan — it has to go completely smooth
- Small ladle, for serving it warm

## Ingredients

- 3 slices bacon, chopped
- ½ cup white onion, finely chopped
- 1 jalapeño, seeded and minced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 cans pinto beans, 425 g each, with their liquid
- ¾ cup chicken broth
- ½ tsp cumin
- ½ tsp chili powder
- ½ tsp kosher salt

## Steps

1. Cook the bacon in the saucepan over medium heat until crisp and
   the fat has rendered out. Leave everything in the pan — the fat is
   the base and the bacon goes into the blender later.
   cue: 6–8 MIN · CRISP, FAT RENDERED

2. Add the onion and jalapeño to the bacon and its fat and cook until
   soft, then stir in the garlic for the last minute.
   cue: 5 MIN · SOFT, GARLIC FRAGRANT

3. Add the beans with their liquid, the broth, the cumin and the
   chili powder. Bring to a simmer and let it go gently so the
   flavours come together.
   cue: 10 MIN · GENTLE SIMMER

4. Blend everything, bacon included, until completely smooth. It
   should pour like a thick soup; thin with more broth if it mounds
   on a spoon. Season with the salt, tasting as you go, because canned
   beans vary.
   cue: COMPLETELY SMOOTH · POURS LIKE THICK SOUP

5. Return it to the pan and hold it on low until serving. It thickens
   as it cools, so loosen with a splash of broth if it sits.
   cue: LOW HEAT · LOOSEN IF IT SITS

## Watchpoints

- Hot liquid in a blender expands. Fill it no more than half full, hold the lid down with a towel, and start on the lowest speed.
- Completely smooth is the target. A dip with bean skins in it is refried beans, which is a different thing.
- Thick soup, not paste. It thickens as it cools and again in the fridge, so stop a shade thinner than you want it at the table.

## Rescues

- **Thick and pasty** — It has cooled, or the beans were drier than usual. Whisk in broth a splash at a time over low heat until it pours again.
- **Thin and watery** — Too much broth, or the bean liquid was thin. Simmer uncovered, stirring, until it coats a spoon; it tightens fast.
- **Grainy, with skins** — Not blended long enough, or a blender that is not up to it. Blend again in smaller batches, a full minute each; a stick blender in the pan will not get there and a jar blender will.
- **Bacon burnt before the fat rendered** [terminal] — The heat was too high. Burnt bacon makes a bitter dip and the fat carries it everywhere. Start again on medium with a cold pan.

## Keeps

- Fridge, 4 days, in a sealed container. It sets firm when cold and that is normal.
- Reheat gently in a pan with a splash of broth, stirring, until it pours again. The microwave works in short bursts with a stir between.
- Freezes for 2 months, though it loosens unevenly on thawing; blend it again warm and it comes back.

## Note

The bean dip at Lupe Tortilla comes out before anything else, in a
small bowl beside the salsa, and it is thinner than any bean dip I
had met before — closer to a soup you scoop with a chip than a
spread. That thinness is the whole character of it, and it is what
every copy gets wrong by leaving out the broth.

For a vegetarian version, skip the bacon, cook the onion in two
tablespoons of oil, and add a quarter teaspoon of smoked paprika for
what the bacon was doing. Home-cooked pinto beans with their broth
are better than canned, if there is a pot of them around.
