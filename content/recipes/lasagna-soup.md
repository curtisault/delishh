## Equipment

- Large Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed pot — a thin base scorches the thick tomato broth where the noodles settle
- Flat-edged wooden spoon, for scraping the bottom while the noodles cook
- Small bowl, for the ricotta topping

## Ingredients

### The soup

- 3 tbsp olive oil, extra-virgin
- 1 yellow onion, large, chopped
- 6 cloves garlic, minced (about 2 tbsp)
- 225 g ground beef
- 225 g bulk Italian sausage, sweet, or links with the casings removed
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- ½ tsp nutmeg, ground
- ¼ tsp red pepper flakes, plus more to taste
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper, freshly ground
- 3 tbsp tomato paste
- 6 cups chicken broth, low-sodium
- 680 g marinara sauce, one jar
- 225 g lasagna noodles, broken into 2–3 cm pieces

### The ricotta topping

- 340 g whole-milk ricotta
- ⅓ cup grated parmesan
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- black pepper, a few grinds

### To finish

- ¼ cup heavy cream
- ½ cup fresh basil, leaves torn, plus more to serve

## Steps

1. Warm the oil in the pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook,
   stirring now and then, until it has softened and gone translucent.
   It should take no color.
   cue: 4–6 MIN · TRANSLUCENT, NO COLOR

2. Add the garlic and stir until it smells sweet and sharp.
   cue: 30–60 SEC · FRAGRANT, NOT BROWNED

3. Add the beef, sausage, oregano, nutmeg, red pepper flakes, 1½ tsp
   salt and ½ tsp pepper. Break the meat into small pieces with the
   spoon and cook until it is browning in places. It does not need to
   cook through; it finishes in the broth.
   cue: 3–5 MIN · BROWNING IN PLACES

4. Stir in the tomato paste and keep stirring until it coats the meat
   and darkens a shade.
   cue: 1 MIN · PASTE COATING THE MEAT

5. Pour in the broth and marinara, raise the heat to medium-high and
   bring it to a boil.
   cue: A FULL ROLLING BOIL

6. Stir in the noodles, pulling apart any that stack, and lower the
   heat to medium-low. Simmer uncovered, stirring often for the first
   few minutes and now and then after, scraping the bottom each time,
   until the noodles are tender and the broth has reduced a little.
   cue: 15–20 MIN · NOODLES TENDER, BROTH A LITTLE LOWER

7. While it simmers, stir the ricotta, parmesan, ¼ tsp salt and a few
   grinds of pepper together in the bowl.
   cue: ONE BOWL, EVEN THROUGHOUT

8. Take the pot off the heat. Stir in the cream and basil, then taste,
   and add salt and red pepper flakes only if it asks for them.
   cue: OFF THE HEAT · TASTE BEFORE SALTING

9. Ladle into shallow bowls, top each with a large spoonful of the
   ricotta and more torn basil, and serve at once.
   cue: A SPOONFUL OF RICOTTA PER BOWL · SERVE AT ONCE

## Watchpoints

- The onion goes translucent and takes no color; browned onion turns the broth sweet and dark.
- Stir the noodles often for the first few minutes, or they stack, clump and catch on the bottom of the thick broth.
- The cream goes in off the heat, so a boiling, acidic broth cannot split it.
- Salt overshoots quietly — the marinara and the sausage both bring their own — so the broth stays low-sodium and the pot is tasted before any more goes in.

## Rescues

- **Too salty** — The marinara and sausage brought more salt than expected. Thin it with unsalted broth or water and add a little more cream.
- **Slick with fat** — The sausage rendered on top of the oil. Skim it off the surface now; next time, spoon it out of the pot before the broth goes in.
- **Noodles clumped or stuck** — They were not stirred early enough. Scrape the bottom, pry the clumps apart with the spoon, and keep stirring until they move freely.
- **A stew by the next day** — The noodles drank the broth overnight. Loosen it with broth as it reheats; it will not return to its first texture.

## Keeps

- Make it and eat it. The noodles go on absorbing the broth after the heat is off, and by the next day the pot is a stew.
- For leftovers on purpose, cook the noodles separately in salted water and keep them apart from the soup; join them in the bowl as each one is reheated.
- The ricotta topping holds, covered, in the fridge.

## Note

Everything I want from a lasagna on a night with no time to build
one. The ricotta on top is the whole point, so don't skip it, and a
cold spoonful sinking into a hot bowl is the best part. Serve it with
garlic bread for the bottom of the bowl. Make only what you'll eat
tonight: the noodles keep soaking after the heat is off, and the
leftovers are a different dish.
