## Equipment

- Wide heavy skillet with a lid — the lid melts the middle before the crust goes too far
- Second wide skillet, for a double onion batch
- Muffin tin, for freezing onion pucks

## Ingredients

### The onions — make these first, or days ahead

- 1.4 kg yellow onions, sliced root to tip — about 5 large; cooks down to 1½ cups
- 3 tbsp butter
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp kosher salt, at the start
- 4 sprigs thyme
- ¼ cup dry sherry or white wine
- 1 tsp sherry vinegar, at the end
- black pepper, plenty

### The sandwich — shred the cheese, never slice it

- 8 slices sourdough, cut 1 cm thick
- 170 g Gruyère, shredded
- 115 g low-moisture mozzarella, shredded
- 4 tsp Dijon, optional — one side only
- 4 tbsp mayonnaise, for the outside, not butter

### The dip — ten minutes, and not really optional

- 2 cups beef broth
- 2 tbsp sherry
- 1 sprig thyme

## Steps

1. Halve each onion through the root and slice each half root to
   tip, about 5 mm. Not into rings — slices cut with the grain hold
   their shape through an hour in the pan; cross-cut rings collapse
   into mush.
   cue: 10 MIN · WITH THE GRAIN, NEVER RINGS

2. Melt the butter and oil in a wide skillet over medium. Add the
   onions and the salt, cover, and let them steam down. Salt early
   pulls the water out fast; the lid gets you past the raw stage
   without browning anything yet.
   cue: 15 MIN · SOFT, LIMP, SWIMMING IN THEIR OWN LIQUID

3. Uncover, drop to medium-low, add the thyme, and cook, stirring
   every five minutes. When the pan goes dry and brown, add a
   tablespoon of water and scrape it clean. Repeat. This
   deglaze-and-scrape cycle is the actual technique — there is no
   honest shortcut, and the baking-soda one buys its color with a
   slick, metallic mush.
   cue: 45 MIN · JAMMY AND MAHOGANY

4. Pour in the sherry and cook it down until the pan is nearly dry.
   Off the heat, stir in the vinegar and plenty of pepper, and pull
   the thyme stems. Onions this sweet need acid or the sandwich
   reads like dessert. Let them cool to warm — screaming-hot onions
   steam the bread from the inside.
   cue: 5 MIN · PAN NEARLY DRY, THEN ACID OFF THE HEAT

5. Stir the two cheeses together. Lay out all eight slices, Dijon on
   four, then cheese on every slice, right against the bread — about
   a third of each sandwich's share per side. Cheese against both
   slices is a waterproof seal; onions touching bare bread make a
   soggy patch every time.
   cue: CHEESE AGAINST BOTH SLICES, ALWAYS

6. Spread a heaping ⅓ cup of onions over the cheese on four slices,
   scatter the last of the cheese on top, and close. The onions ride
   in a melted seam instead of sliding out the side on the first
   bite.
   cue: ONIONS BETWEEN TWO WALLS OF CHEESE

7. Spread mayonnaise edge to edge on both outer faces — it browns
   evenly where butter pools and scorches, and does not taste like
   mayonnaise afterward. Griddle over medium-low, covered, until
   deep brown and the cheese runs at the edges. Press once, gently.
   cue: 4–5 MIN A SIDE · DEEP BROWN, CHEESE AT THE EDGES

8. While they cook, simmer the broth, sherry, and thyme in the onion
   pan, scraping up whatever is stuck. Rest the sandwiches two
   minutes — cutting straight off the heat dumps molten cheese onto
   the board — then cut on the diagonal and serve with the broth in
   a mug.
   cue: REST 2 MIN · UNTIL THE CHEESE STAYS PUT

## Watchpoints

- The onions are the whole job; the sandwich takes ten minutes. Both timelines on the plate at once means the onions came from the freezer.
- Bread at 1 cm. Soft sandwich bread collapses under the onions; much thicker leaves a cold seam of cheese in the center.
- Build to order, always. An assembled sandwich waiting around is a soggy one.

## Rescues

- **Soggy patch in the middle** — Onions touched bare bread. A few minutes in a hot oven firms this one; next build, seal both slices with cheese first.
- **Crust dark before the middle melts** — Heat too high, or no lid. Drop to medium-low and cover; the lid is what melts the seam.
- **Greasy, with oil pooling** — An all-Gruyère filling. It has the best flavor and it greases; the mozzarella is there to hold it together.
- **Onions watery out of storage** — They went into the container warm. Cook the water back off in a dry pan over low before they touch bread.

## Keeps

- The onions are the only part that keeps. Fridge, 5 days, packed tight in a jar under a thin film of oil.
- Freezer, 3 months: portion ⅓ cup into a muffin tin, freeze solid, pop the pucks into a bag. One puck, one sandwich.
- Thaw a puck in a dry pan over low — never the microwave. The water has to cook back off before it meets bread.
- For a crowd: build them all, mayo both faces, bake on a preheated sheet pan at 220 °C / 425 °F, 7 minutes a side. Six fit a half sheet.
- Hold finished sandwiches on a rack in a 95 °C / 200 °F oven — never stacked, never wrapped. Reheat from the fridge at 200 °C / 400 °F on a rack, 6–8 minutes. A microwave turns the crust to leather in under a minute, and there is no coming back.

## Note

Nobody caramelizes onions on a Tuesday. So don't — six pounds on a
Sunday across two wide skillets, and this becomes a fifteen-minute
sandwich for the next three months. The salty broth is what makes it
read as French onion soup rather than an onion sandwich; store-bought
beef broth simmered five minutes in the onion pan with a splash of
sherry is genuinely enough. Gruyère with mozzarella is the house
blend — Comté and fontina when it should be softer and sweeter,
Swiss and provolone when the cheese drawer is thin.
