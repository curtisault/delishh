## Equipment

- Blender
- Flexible spatula, for getting all of it out of the jar

## Ingredients

- 2 cups vanilla ice cream, softened slightly — one without gelatin in it
- ½ cup milk
- ¼ cup chocolate syrup
- 2 tbsp malted milk powder, optional — the malt is what makes it taste like the drive-through
- 1 tbsp cocoa powder

## Steps

1. Everything into the blender — ice cream, milk, syrup, malt powder,
   cocoa. No order matters.
   cue: EVERYTHING IN, LID ON

2. Blend on medium speed until it comes together.
   cue: 30–45 S · UNTIL SMOOTH AND CREAMY

3. Check the body: thick but spoonable, like soft-serve. Too thick,
   add a splash more milk; too thin, another spoonful of ice cream
   and a short pulse.
   cue: SPOONABLE, NOT POURABLE

4. Into glasses, spoons in, immediately. It is melting from the
   moment the blender stops.
   cue: SERVE AT ONCE

## Watchpoints

- Stop the blender the moment it turns smooth. Every second past that is friction warming it toward a milkshake.
- Vanilla ice cream, not chocolate. The chocolate comes from the syrup and the cocoa; chocolate ice cream lands somewhere else entirely.

## Rescues

- **Melted to a milkshake** — Over-blended, or the ice cream started too soft. Ten to fifteen minutes in the freezer and one stir brings the body back.
- **Thin and watery-tasting** — The milk crowded out the ice cream. Add ice cream a spoonful at a time with the shortest pulses the blender has.

## Keeps

- It does not keep. Frozen, it sets into a brick; thawed, it separates into sweet milk. Make it, eat it.

## Note

The malted milk powder is the sleeper — the syrup and cocoa get you a
chocolate shake, but the malt is what makes it taste like the one from
the drive-through. Swapping the regular milk for sweetened condensed
milk makes it richer if that is the mood, and leaving out the syrup
and cocoa entirely gets you the vanilla one.
