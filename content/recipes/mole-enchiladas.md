## Equipment

- 23 × 33 cm baking dish — two rows of four fill it
- Wide pot, for the mole
- Small saucepan and a small dish, for the queso core
- Dry griddle or comal, for the tortillas

## Ingredients

### The mole — from the jar, woken up

- 235 g mole paste, one jar — Doña María or similar
- 2 tbsp lard or neutral oil
- 4 cups chicken broth, hot — not water
- 45 g Mexican chocolate, chopped
- 1 tbsp peanut butter or almond butter
- 1 chipotle in adobo, minced
- 1 tsp cider vinegar
- ¼ tsp cinnamon
- salt and brown sugar, to correct

### The queso core — make first, it has to chill

- 3 tbsp butter
- 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 355 ml evaporated milk, one can
- 225 g white American cheese, torn deli slices — not singles
- 170 g Monterey Jack, shredded
- 115 g can diced green chiles, drained hard
- ½ tsp kosher salt

### Assembly

- 8 fresh flour tortillas, 25 cm
- 4 cups shredded chicken thigh
- 1½ cups Monterey Jack, shredded

### To finish — at the table only

- ¾ cup queso fresco or cotija, crumbled
- ½ white onion, thin rings
- ½ cup cilantro
- crema and lime wedges

## Steps

1. Build the queso first. Melt the butter, whisk in the flour, and
   cook one minute. Add the evaporated milk in a stream and simmer
   until thick. Off the heat, melt in both cheeses, then the drained
   chiles and salt. Evaporated milk and deli American are doing
   chemistry, not cutting corners — the emulsifying salts in both
   are what keep the core from splitting.
   cue: 10 MIN · THICK, THEN CHEESE OFF THE HEAT

2. Spread the queso 1 cm deep in a small dish, press plastic onto
   the surface, and refrigerate. This is the whole trick: warm queso
   runs out of the tortilla and pools in the pan; cold, it stays put
   and melts back into a seam in the oven.
   cue: 2 H · SCOOPS LIKE COLD MASHED POTATO

3. Heat the lard in a wide pot. Add the mole paste and mash it
   around the hot pan until it darkens and smells toasty rather than
   raw. The jar is a concentrate, not a finished sauce — frying it
   in fat is the step that separates "from a jar" from "from
   scratch" on the plate.
   cue: 4 MIN · DARKER, AND TOASTY OVER RAW

4. Whisk in the hot broth a cup at a time until smooth, then add the
   chocolate, nut butter, chipotle, and cinnamon. Simmer until it
   coats a spoon and mounds slightly. The label says water; water
   gets a thin, sweet, one-note sauce — broth gives it the savory
   backbone the paste does not have.
   cue: 15 MIN · COATS A SPOON, MOUNDS SLIGHTLY

5. Taste. Add the vinegar, then salt in pinches until it stops
   tasting flat, and sugar only if it is fighting you. Jarred paste
   is already sweet and already salted — season at the end or
   overshoot both.
   cue: VINEGAR FIRST, THEN SALT IN PINCHES

6. If the tortillas came uncooked, cook them on the dry griddle
   until puffed and spotted; if already cooked, still warm them
   fifteen seconds a side. A warm flour tortilla rolls; a cold one
   splits down the seam and unrolls itself in the pan.
   cue: 6 MIN · PUFFED AND SPOTTED, OR 15 S A SIDE

7. Lay a stripe of cold queso across the lower third of each
   tortilla, ½ cup of chicken over it, then roll tight and set
   seam-down in the dish. Queso against the tortilla, chicken on top
   of it — a core buried in the middle stays molten instead of
   leaking out the ends.
   cue: 10 MIN · TWO ROWS OF FOUR, SEAM-DOWN

8. Ladle the mole over, scatter the Jack, cover, and bake at
   175 °C / 350 °F for 30 minutes, then uncover for 5. Rest 10
   minutes and garnish at the table. Sauce it right before it goes
   in — flour tortillas sitting under wet mole go slack in about
   twenty minutes.
   cue: 30 MIN COVERED + 5 UNCOVERED · REST 10

## Watchpoints

- The queso chill is not optional. Two hours minimum, until it scoops like cold mashed potato — a warm core is the difference between a seam of cheese and a pan of soup.
- Taste the jar before committing the chocolate and sugar. Some pastes are chile-forward and some are almost candy; a sweet brand needs the vinegar and maybe a second chipotle instead.
- Sauce and bake in one motion. The pan does not wait around assembled.

## Rescues

- **Sauce thin and one-note** — It was loosened with water, or not simmered down. Back on the heat until it mounds; a spoon of nut butter deepens it.
- **Sauce sweet and cloying** — A candy-leaning jar. The cider vinegar and a second minced chipotle pull it back.
- **Rolls split down the seam** — The tortillas went in cold. Warm every one, even the pre-cooked kind.
- **Cheese ran out the ends** — The core went in warm, or the queso stripe reached the edges. Chill it hard and keep the filling to the lower third.

## Keeps

- Freeze the parts, never the assembled pan — flour tortillas go slack under wet mole and a frozen pan bakes up gummy, the rolls dissolving into the sauce.
- Mole: flat in quart bags, 2 cups each, air pressed out — 4 months. It freezes better than anything else on this card.
- Queso: a block with plastic pressed to the surface, foil over it — 2 months. Thaw in the fridge; it goes into the tortillas cold, no re-melting.
- Chicken: bagged with a few spoons of broth so it does not dry out — 3 months.
- Fridge make-ahead: mole holds a week, queso 5 days. Both made on Sunday turn this into a 25-minute Wednesday, which is the actual point of the shortcut.
- To freeze a whole pan anyway: switch to fresh corn tortillas, dip each in warm mole before rolling, then 3 months frozen and bake at 175 °C / 350 °F, 60 minutes covered plus 15.

## Note

The finish is structural, not decorative — mole is dense and
sweet-bitter, and the raw onion rings, queso fresco, cilantro, and a
hard squeeze of lime are what keep a plate of it from flattening out.
Fresh flour tortillas are the right call for eating tonight; they are
softer, they hold more, and a fresh one from the tortilleria counter
beats any corn tortilla out of a bag. They are the wrong call for the
freezer, which is why this card freezes parts.
